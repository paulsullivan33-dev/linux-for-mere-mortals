# Linux for Mere Mortals

Linux is the operating system running most of the world's servers,
most phones (Android is Linux underneath), most smart TVs, and the
top 500 supercomputers — all 500 of them. If you've ever wondered
what's actually going on inside that black terminal window, this
guide is for you.

No CS degree required. No assumptions about what you already know.
Ideas first, then commands you can type yourself.

## What this guide covers

- **What Linux is** — and what it isn't (it's not a company, and it's
  not one single product)
- **Getting a Linux to play with** — without risking your main computer
- **The terminal** — the black window, the prompt, and why people who
  could use a mouse choose not to
- **Files and directories** — getting around, making things, moving
  things, and the one command that can ruin your day
- **Permissions** — who can read, write, and run what, and why
- **Finding things** — searching files and asking for help
- **Processes** — what's running, and how to stop it
- **Installing software** — package managers, the app store that came
  before app stores
- **Remote machines** — logging into another computer over the network
- **Disks and services** — where your space went, and what starts at boot
- **Troubleshooting** — reading error messages instead of fearing them

## What it doesn't cover

This guide won't make you a system administrator. It won't cover
kernel internals, shell scripting (see [Bash for Mere
Mortals](https://paulsullivan33-dev.github.io/bash-for-mere-mortals/)),
or running servers in production. It covers the 20% of Linux that
handles 80% of daily life at the command line.

## How to use this guide

Every command shown is meant to be typed. **Check:** blocks show what
success looks like. Anything that can delete or break things is marked
**Careful:** — read those twice.

Examples were tested on Ubuntu 24.04. Your output will differ in
details (usernames, exact sizes), but the shape will match.
